## Resolved research open questions and assumptions (naming and mechanics choices, recorded here; relayed by plan 16-07)

- Open Q1 (Custom ergonomics): a public `TierSelector.Custom(picker)` constructor (ROUT-01, literally callable) plus a
  companion `invoke(picker) { id; capabilities }` builder, matching `TierPolicy { }`. The Builder var is named
  `capabilities`, not research's `providers`, for consistency with the strategy builders. No default-argument
  constructor (ApiShapeTest).
- Open Q2 (fallback cause token): none in v1.1. D-04 chose the single `router_fallback` code, and the selection record
  stays small. A cause can be added later without breaking anything.
- Open Q3 (docs): prose and table rows only in this phase, with no new Kotlin fence or DocSnippets region. Phase 19
  (DOC-02) adds the compiled snippet.
- Open Q4 (picked tier is an unavailable on-device-only tier): it stays in `eligible`, and `runTier` fails loudly, as
  Linear does. This is moot in v1.1 because no on-device tier ships.
- A3: when the Router skips because exactly one LLM tier is eligible, it records no `router_fallback` and opens no
  selection record. It is not a fallback: the single tier is the Linear start.
- A4: the default ids are `start_tier_picker` (Custom) and `start_tier_router` (Router). Both are overridable and
  collision-checked.
- A5 (refined): the record is `StartTierSelection`, the policy field is `pickerTimeoutMillis`, the Router input is
  `Router.Builder.tierDescriptions`, and the event is `PipelineEvent.StartTierSelected`. Research proposed
  `TierPicked`. It is renamed because the event also fires on a fallback, where nothing was picked but a start tier
  was still selected. All these names are renamable until the v1.1.0 tag, and plan 16-07 relays them. Only
  `tiersBypassed` is locked (D-09).
- D-08 "small maxTokens" (option text): the Router's cap is `policy.maxTokensPerTurn`, like every tier. Limits come
  from policy, and NoHardCodedConstantsTest forbids a limit constant outside the owner files. The answer is kept tiny
  by its shape instead: one forced call with one enum string. Relayed as an open item in 16-07.

## Edge assumptions (no SPEC.md)

- ROUT-01: the picker is called at most once per command, and only after the zero-call head handed over. Its
  `eligible` list is non-empty and holds only tiers with at least one declared provider, in ladder order.
- ROUT-02: a zero-call tier in the middle of the ladder is never in `eligible` and is never pre-run. Picking past it
  bypasses it, and it is not counted in `tiersBypassed`.
- ROUT-03: a pick that is not in `eligible` (null, unknown, a zero-call id, a throw, a timeout) starts the walk at the
  first LLM tier (index 0 of the remaining tiers). The outcome type is whatever that Linear walk yields.
- ROUT-04: "no eligible LLM tier" is decided after policy and after the head ran. A whole-ladder refusal is a Failed
  before any tier and records no `router_fallback`.
- ROUT-05: with no selector set, the walk, trace, codes and events are exactly v1.0.1's (this plan's test).
